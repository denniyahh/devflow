## Task 3 is the one-way gate for 23-15, and what it does and does not re-ask

`REVERSIBILITY_GATES` is on, and 23-15's acceptance run carries a `one-way`
reversibility rating — it performs a real merge into `develop`, a real version
bump, and a real changelog commit on the operator's primary repository. The
reversibility rules require a decision checkpoint before that door. **Task 3 is
that checkpoint.** It sits in the immediately preceding wave, and 23-15 depends
on this plan, so the gate precedes the door across the plan boundary rather than
inside a single file. A structural validator that scans one plan at a time
cannot see this and will flag 23-15 — that flag is a tooling limitation, not a
missing gate.

What Task 3 does **not** re-ask is D-07's risk acceptance. That is settled:
`23-CONTEXT.md` records the operator considering and declining a self-dogfood
refusal guard, accepting the risk on the stated grounds that a genuine hands-off
proof is the point of the phase; `23-ACCEPTANCE-SETUP.md` Task 4 records the
`PROCEED`; and `ROADMAP.md`'s 2026-07-26 gap-closure decision directs the re-run
in as many words — *"the gap plan should ship the guard **and** re-run the
acceptance attempt against Phase 24."* Re-litigating a settled risk acceptance
produces exactly the checkpoint fatigue that makes gates stop being read.

What Task 3 **does** decide is narrower and genuinely open: launch now against
this tree and this binary, hold, or retarget. Three real options with different
consequences — which is what makes it a decision rather than a formality.
