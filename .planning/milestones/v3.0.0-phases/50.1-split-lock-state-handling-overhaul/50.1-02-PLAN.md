  <behavior>
    - `supervisor::runtime::tests::a_persistent_accept_error_backs_off_and_is_logged`: a `Control` whose accept seam always returns `io::Error::from_raw_os_error(libc::EMFILE)`, with one client connected so `poll` keeps reporting the listener readable, runs for one second and is then told to quit; the seam's attempt counter is at most 25 (the backoff starts at 10 ms and doubles to a 1 s cap, so a correct loop makes at most about 7 attempts), and the error-log hook was called at least once and no more often than the attempts. Against today's `accept_all` the same test counts thousands of attempts and fails
    - `supervisor::runtime::tests::a_would_block_accept_returns_without_backing_off`: with the seam returning `WouldBlock` on the first call, `accept_all` returns at once and the backoff state stays at its initial value

  <action>
Give `Control` an accept seam: a field holding `fn(&UnixListener) -> io::Result<(UnixStream, std::os::unix::net::SocketAddr)>`
(production value: `UnixListener::accept`), and an `accept_backoff: Cell<Duration>` starting at `ACCEPT_BACKOFF_START`
(10 ms) with `ACCEPT_BACKOFF_CAP` (1 s) as named constants. In `accept_all`'s persistent-error arm: log
`"supervisor accept failed: {error}; retrying in {backoff:?}"` through a private `log_accept_error` (the test counts its
calls through a `#[cfg(test)]` counter), sleep the current backoff, double it up to the cap, and return. On a successful
accept reset the backoff to the start value. `WouldBlock` and `Interrupted` keep today's behaviour. The sleep happens on
the control thread, which only serves pings and shutdowns, so a one-second worst case delays a ping by at most that
(state this in the doc comment). Write both tests red first against the unchanged loop; record the attempt count of the
RED run in the SUMMARY.
  </action>
