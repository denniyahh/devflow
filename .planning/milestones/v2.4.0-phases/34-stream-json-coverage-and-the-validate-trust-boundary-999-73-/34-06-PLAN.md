<objective>
Make this phase's test suite survive the widening that plan 34-05 must perform, so 34-05 spends its
budget on evidence rather than on an unbudgeted repair discovered mid-run under a live-capture time
constraint.

Purpose: plan 34-02 reported that widening `STREAM_JSON_STAGES` to all five stages breaks 14
`pipeline_launch::` tests. Re-measured at this phase's current HEAD — after 34-04 also modified
`pipeline_launch.rs` — the count is **42 failed across four modules**, and the composition is the
finding: **41 are `PoisonError`, exactly 1 is a genuine failure.** The arithmetic is exact
(42 failed − 41 poison = 1 root).

The single root failure is `pipeline_launch::tests::launch_stage_persists_monitor_pid_for_reload`.
Its fixture builds `State::new(...)` without pinning a stage, so under widening its stage joins the
stream path, `canary_gate` invokes the real `ClaudeCanaryLauncher`, and delivery cannot be confirmed
in a test environment. It panics while holding `ENV_MUTEX`, which poisons the lock for the other 41.

That amplifier is not a discovery — `test_support.rs:267` already documents it verbatim: "the panic
poisons `ENV_MUTEX`, turning every subsequent `ENV_MUTEX.lock().unwrap()` into a `PoisonError` panic.
One legible failing assertion becomes a cascade across the whole binary." It is documented and
unfixed. It is also why 34-02 read 14 and this orchestrator read 42 for the same one defect, and why
34-04's flake report described a ~30-test `PoisonError` cascade from a single concurrency test.

Output: a poison-tolerant `ENV_MUTEX` accessor at all 58 call sites, the stage-membership premise
removed from every test that carried one incidentally, and a recorded widened-suite measurement of
`0 failed` reported together with the control that proves the measurement can still go red.
</objective>

pub(crate) fn env_lock() -> std::sync::MutexGuard<'static, ()> {
    ENV_MUTEX.lock().unwrap_or_else(|poisoned| poisoned.into_inner())
